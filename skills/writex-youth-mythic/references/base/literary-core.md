# Literary Core v2

## Perennial human question

A valid perennial question must satisfy all of these:

- recur meaningfully across different eras or social conditions;
- protect a real human value on both sides;
- have no single standard answer that dissolves the conflict;
- become concrete through this character's actual life rather than remain philosophical commentary;
- be capable of forcing an irreversible or costly choice.

Weak: "Is immortality ethical?"

Stronger: "If love partly means accepting that two people cannot possess each other's whole life, what happens when one person can refuse the ending that gives the other's life its shape?"

## Character engine

For the POV character always establish:

- surface desire;
- hidden desire;
- shame;
- self-deception;
- attachment/dependency;
- what fact or choice could break the self-deception;
- the impossible choice and the losses on both branches.

A character should often protect the very thing that prevents them from getting what they consciously want.

## Chinese emotional priorities

When organically relevant, test for pressure from:

- family as emotional home rather than only biological relation;
- intergenerational duty, sacrifice, and expectation;
- home-place, rootedness, return, and leaving;
- face, dignity, reputation, and the gaze of familiar others;
- relational obligation, favors, and reciprocity;
- collective belonging versus individual selfhood.

Do not mechanically insert all six. Choose only pressures that change decisions.
