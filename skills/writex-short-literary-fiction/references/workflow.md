# Short-fiction workflow

## 1. Establish the story kernel

Define four linked elements:

1. A perennial human question with defensible values on both sides.
2. A character's shame, desire, and self-deception.
3. A choice that cannot preserve both valued outcomes.
4. A specific life situation that makes the conflict observable through work, family, place, money, body, habit, or relationship.

Reject kernels that are only a topic, twist, atmosphere, or technology premise. Prefer a conflict that would still matter if the speculative device were removed.

## 2. Design the short-story arc

Map one compact four-phase movement:

- Opening: establish ordinary pressure, character strategy, and a concrete disturbance.
- Development: make the strategy increasingly costly; deepen rather than merely repeat the conflict.
- Climax: force the impossible choice through action, not explanation alone.
- Ending: show the paid cost and changed meaning. Close the story's emotional contract without explaining everything.

Do not import long-form machinery such as volumes, distant chapter promises, or rolling-horizon planning.

## 3. Build scenes and a technique budget

Each scene needs an entry condition, active desire, resistance, turn, and exit change. Remove scenes that only deliver background or repeat an emotion.

Choose no more than a few active techniques for the story and usually one primary technique per beat:

- emotional delay;
- subtext;
- character-specific metaphor;
- visual or object-led action;
- retrospective knowledge gap;
- macro/micro scale shift;
- motif return with transformed meaning.

Plan both use and restraint. A technique is successful only when it changes the reader's experience of the character or choice.

## 4. Run three-draft competition

Create three candidates from the same factual core and constraints. Give each a distinct dramatic strategy, for example:

- candidate A prioritizes character behavior and subtext;
- candidate B prioritizes scene clarity and causal pressure;
- candidate C prioritizes emotional release and structural surprise.

Remove author/model labels before review. Do not let one candidate inherit another's score.

## 5. Review, select, and revise

Review every candidate with evidence from the text using [review-rubric.md](review-rubric.md). Select the strongest whole-story strategy; do not assemble a paragraph collage unless the combined causality and voice are rechecked.

Turn diagnoses into a bounded revision plan:

- preserve what already works;
- name the scene or passage to change;
- state the intended reader effect;
- prohibit unrelated rewrites;
- review the revised story again from zero.

Stop for human review when the central choice, ending meaning, personal facts, or publication disclosure would materially change.

## 6. Prepare the submission draft

Proofread title, paragraphing, punctuation, names, chronology, and word count. When a target platform is named, verify its current length, file-format, exclusivity, contact-information, and AI-disclosure rules from authoritative sources.

Keep the manuscript primary. Submission notes should describe the story and participation honestly without turning the pitch into a product demonstration.
