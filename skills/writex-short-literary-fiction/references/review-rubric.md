# Evidence-based review rubric

Judge the whole story and cite concrete evidence. Scores support diagnosis; they do not replace it.

## Core dimensions

- Human question: the conflict protects real value on both sides and cannot be solved by a slogan.
- Character truth: behavior grows from desire, shame, self-deception, history, and current pressure.
- Difficult choice: the climax forces action; the chosen value causes a visible loss or cost.
- Specific life: setting, work, objects, habits, money, family, and social relations make the story inhabited rather than generic.
- Narrative control: information, pacing, point of view, scene order, and ending pressure are deliberate.
- Language and voice: images and diction belong to this narrator or character; polished sentences do not flatten individual voice.
- Emotional release: feeling arrives through accumulated action and detail rather than explanatory declarations.
- Originality and necessity: the premise, scenes, and ending do more than reproduce a familiar template or twist.

## Veto conditions

A candidate cannot be submission-ready when any of these remain:

- the central choice has no meaningful cost;
- a character knows information they could not know;
- a late twist replaces rather than fulfills the emotional arc;
- the ending explains the theme instead of completing the action;
- narrative technique is conspicuously overstacked or repeatedly announces emotion;
- personal or factual claims are presented without a reliable basis;
- the text closely imitates a named living author's distinctive expression;
- platform requirements or AI participation are knowingly misrepresented.

## Review output

For each candidate provide:

1. strongest achieved effect and its evidence;
2. highest-severity failure and its evidence;
3. whether the story's choice and cost are complete;
4. technique-control diagnosis: absent, underused, controlled, or overused;
5. recommendation: select, revise, or reject;
6. the smallest revision that could materially improve the story.
